How the Omi Pendant Works

A visual guide to what's inside the pendant, where your audio goes, and what can go wrong. No electronics knowledge needed.

What's inside

Take it apart and you get a stack of layers about the size of a large coin. The parts that matter for behaviour are the circuit board and the battery.

How it works, one picture at a time

Each card shows one behaviour, plus where it happens on the real board.

1

Sound becomes blocks

While awake, the microphones capture sound and the brain chops it into tiny compressed blocks, about the size of a text message each.

On the boardThe two microphones sit on opposite edges. The brain chip does the chopping.
2

Blocks fly to the phone

Over Bluetooth, the blocks go to the Omi app. The app sends them to the cloud, which turns them into words. All the smart stuff happens there, not on the pendant.

On the boardThe radio is built into the brain chip. A spring contact passes the signal to the metal case, which works as the antenna.
3

No phone? Save it for later

When the phone is out of range, the pendant stores the blocks in its own memory instead, as long as its clock is set. The light turns red, but that's normal, nothing is wrong.

On the boardThe memory chip is on the underside of the board.
4

Phone's back: catch up

When the phone reconnects, the app quietly downloads what was saved. Those conversations show up later, sometimes hours later. That's expected.

On the boardThe same memory chip, read back over Bluetooth.
5

Quiet room? Take a nap

After about 10 seconds of silence, recording pauses to save battery, and the microphone's own low-power detector keeps listening. A loud enough sound restarts recording. Quiet sounds, and the first moment of speech, can be missed.

On the boardThe left microphone has a "wake up" wire back to the brain. It listens for sound on its own.
6

Connected but not listening

Sometimes the phone stays connected but stops asking for audio. iPhones do this to apps in the background. The pendant throws the blocks away and the light stays blue, so nobody notices. This is how a real meeting was lost. A fix is in progress.

On the boardThis happens in the brain chip's software, so the fix is a firmware update.
7

Read the light

Solid blueConnected. Not proof that audio is reaching the phone.
Solid redNo phone. Saving to memory, which is fine.
Blinking redIts clock isn't set, usually because it has never connected to the app. It can't save anything offline yet.
Green + blue/redCharging. The other colour shows connection.
Solid greenCharged.

Users read blue as "working" and red as "broken". Both can be wrong. Making the light honest is on the list for the next firmware release.

On the boardTwo colour lights sit either side of the button, in the middle of the board.
8

Know the limits

Battery
~17–20 h if it never naps
1 day
Memory
~1.5 days of audio
Field logs
none: the pendant doesn't report problems

Bars share one scale of 36 hours, and the tick marks one day. Battery runs out before memory does. These are estimates from the design and past measurements, not field data.

On the boardThe battery isn't on the board. It is soldered to two pads, and this small chip charges it from the dock.
9

Getting a fix onto the pendant

The case is sealed, so normal updates arrive only through the app, and only when someone accepts them. Engineers can also reflash by wire, but that means opening the case. Many never do. Every old version stays out in the world, and the app has to keep working with all of them. App and cloud fixes reach everyone within days. Firmware fixes trickle out.

On the boardNew firmware first downloads into this small separate chip. On restart, the pendant swaps it in.

The one rule: audio is never silently lost.Every block must reach the phone or be saved for later, unless the user turned the pendant off or muted it on purpose. Saving phone battery can change when blocks are sent, never whether they survive. (Internally this is called INV-CAP-1.)

Before you decide anything about the pendant

Does it change when the phone stops listening?Then it must not open a gap where blocks go in the bin.
Does it need new firmware?Expect a slow, partial rollout. If the app or cloud can solve it, start there.
How will we know it worked?The pendant reports nothing by itself. Decide what to measure before you ship.
What does the user see when it fails?An honest "this part is missing" beats a clean timeline with hidden holes.
What does it cost in battery?A full day of battery is the hardest limit the device has.
