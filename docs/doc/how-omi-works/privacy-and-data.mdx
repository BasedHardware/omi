Omi records conversations. That is the product, and it makes the data model worth understanding before you build on it. This page is the short, stable version for builders; the formal documents are [Privacy](/doc/info/Privacy) and [Disclaimer](/doc/info/Disclaimer), and end-user controls are documented in the [help center](https://help.omi.me).

## What exists, and who can see it

Every account holds **conversations** (transcripts and, depending on settings, audio), **memories** (extracted facts), **action items**, **goals**, and **people** (recognized speakers). Every API surface (the app, Developer API, MCP, CLI, and installed apps) is scoped to the signed-in account.

Sharing is the one exception, and you start it. A shared conversation or task link can be opened without signing in. It returns a limited view: for a conversation, its summary and transcript; for a task, its description and due date.

Apps you install can receive the data you grant them (for example conversation webhooks or chat tools); app authors see only what flows to their endpoints.

## Where it lives

- Conversations, memories, and metadata are stored server-side in your account and sent over encrypted connections. Accounts can turn on enhanced data protection for additional encryption of stored content. The [Privacy policy](/doc/info/Privacy) is the authoritative description.
- The desktop app additionally keeps a local database on your machine (see [Omi for Mac](/onboarding/omi-macos)).
- Keeping raw audio is your choice: **Store Audio on Phone** and **Store Audio on Cloud** control whether recordings are kept, and where.

## Your controls

- **Export**: the app exports your conversations as JSON.
- **Delete**: delete individual conversations, or your whole account, from the app.
- **Bring your own keys**: [BYOK and custom transcription](/doc/get_started/byok_and_custom_transcription) let you route transcription and models through providers you choose, including on-device and self-hosted transcription.
- **Scoped API keys**: developer keys and MCP grants can be read-only and per-scope, and can be revoked at any time.

## If you build on Omi

Treat webhook payloads and API responses as your users' sensitive data: send it over TLS, keep it no longer than you need, and say clearly in your app listing what your app receives. The [app review guidelines](/doc/developer/apps/Submitting) enforce the basics.
