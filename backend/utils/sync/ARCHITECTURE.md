# Sync utilities

This package owns uploaded-audio sync admission, decoding, transcription orchestration, persistence fencing, and playback reconstruction.

## Boundaries

- `pipeline.py` coordinates job/run leases, segment processing, persistence fences, and terminal outcomes. `assignment_errors.py` distinguishes terminal user authority from corrupt/mismatched intake.
- `files.py`, `content_id.py`, and `capture_manifest.py` normalize uploads and identities. Capture assignment never grants fresh-lane provenance.
- `capture.py` derives retry-stable incoming IDs from the VAD-segment timestamp. VAD exports speech segments only; empty VAD/STT creates no conversation or bridge and bills no speech.
- `lanes.py`, `backfill.py`, and `rate_limit.py` classify work and enforce admission policy. `uid_sequencer.py` dispatches accepted backfill jobs through the Firestore owner/pending registry in `database/sync_backfill_sequencer.py`; it owns no STT or conversation writes. See `backend/docs/runbooks/sync-two-lane.md` for lease, cutover, and monitoring details.
- `merge_audio.py` and `merge_dedupe.py` contain deterministic merge helpers; `playback.py` reconstructs audio artifacts.
- `provenance.py` and `telemetry.py` provide bounded attribution and operational labels.
- `recording_lineage.py` (default) and `recording_session_target.py` (kill-switch path) choose explicit live targets for safety-WAL uploads that carry a recording id; they only read, and intake stays in `assignment.py`.

Keep HTTP routing and database implementations outside this package. Changes must preserve run-lease, durable result, and conversation-persistence fences in `pipeline.py`.

## Capture assignment

The 2026-09-19 incident was **sync adopting live-flap stubs**: failed live STT caused reconnects about every 35 seconds, creating empty conversations with near-zero extent. The legacy timestamp lookup chose a different nearest stub for each 60-second WAL, even within an ordered job. The observed jobs were legacy backfill without explicit targets or turnstile timeouts. Cross-job lookup/create races addressed by #15106 are a separate, older class.

`lifecycle.ingest_sync_conversation` admits uploads through `database.conversations.assign_sync_conversation`. `assignment.py` computes connected capture components in one Firestore transaction, using codecs supplied by the database adapter. Every transactional read precedes writes. Decode/storage errors propagate; there is no uncoordinated create fallback.

`assignment_index.py` stores metadata-only UTC day buckets under `users/{uid}/sync_assignment`. Every occupied day indexes the component. Buckets do not evict entries; Firestore size limits fail visibly. The old `recent` document remains a bounded migration hint and serialization fence, alongside the legacy timestamp-query hint. Pre-index history is not comprehensively backfilled. `_OrderedTurnstile` reduces bridge work within a job; correctness does not depend on it.

`utils/conversation_continuity.py` owns the gap predicate: an uncovered gap **at least 120 seconds** splits. Both paths measure speech silence. Sync uses the VAD-segment origin through the last word end, exactly as before; no quiet-file coverage expands that interval. Source, device ID and lock state form disjoint partitions. Any retained `sync_capture_id` is provenance only; an explicit existing target is authoritative independently of temporal assignment. Missing device ID is its own partition, never a wildcard joining two known devices. Every provenance-compatible, non-deleted explicit target retains its ID, including an empty live record or an existing sync row. Fresh admission can verify server capture proof before any live transcript exists; emptiness is not evidence to reject that target. Missing or tombstoned explicit targets use ordinary temporal assignment, subject to independent retry-lineage deletion fences. Timestamp hints never adopt live rows, regardless of content, status or discarded flag. Explicit live targets acquire `sync_live_target` and remain excluded from automatic bridges after sync adds content and revisions. Shared, photo-bearing or user-curated sync rows also stay intact; they cannot donate content or change identity automatically.

A live conversation folded into its predecessor by smart merge (`utils/conversations/smart_merge.py`) is a redirect tombstone marked `smart_merge.role=donor`. An explicit target that is such a donor resolves through `sync_merged_into` to its survivor, which then acts as the explicit live target (text-deduplicated repair audio, no temporal re-creation of the donor); a deleted survivor supersedes the chunk. Sync bridge donors keep the temporal fallback.

Without an authoritative explicit target, when matches exist the survivor is the existing row with earliest `started_at`, then smallest ID on a tie. Incoming chunk IDs create records only when nothing matches. Ordinary append keeps its visible ID; genuine bridges retain redirect tombstones. For unattended temporal intake, partition and absolute content converge across permutations, not the survivor ID. Explicit targets retain their ID and lifecycle authority; user-deleted targets are never reused. This does not authenticate client provenance. Transcript ranges remain absolute through rebasing; `finished_at` is the last word end; the existing duration helper controls displayed speech duration.

Bridge writes increment survivor and donor `sync_content_revision`. Processors reject deleted rows and stale revisions. `sync_merged_from` persists ancestry with the transcript, so `bridge.py` can replay external effects after a failed attempt. The only completion call is after segment audio persistence in `process_segment`; transactional lifecycle intake has no external cleanup. It calls the public `retract_sync_bridge_source` and `copy_sync_bridge_audio` seams in `merge_conversations.py`; those fix retention and strict-copy policy while keeping the general merge/delete machinery private. `database/sync_bridges.py` checkpoints `sync_bridge_cleaned_revision` on each tombstone with a revision compare-and-set, and later appends skip completed retraction. `sync_bridge_audio_target` tracks the copy destination: later bridges and late donor audio copy without repeating completed retraction. Donor retraction does not take the exclusive per-account destructive gate; if that gate is already held, retraction is deferred (logged `event=sync_bridge outcome=deferred`) and retried on the next append via the missing receipt. Copy and checkpoint failures still propagate to the job retry path; concurrent attempts may repeat effects until a receipt commits. Original audio and donor tombstones remain while uploads can finish; user/source deletion purges retained sources through `delete_conversation_with_sync_sources`, called by the deletion service and developer delete endpoint; the raw database hard-delete primitive does no external orchestration. In-flight donor audio is recopied when its worker finishes; the previous copy receipt is cleared first, so failed late copying remains pending on retry without a transient source hint. There is no independent cleanup scheduler: recovery depends on a retried job or later intake. Search-index writes retain their existing best-effort semantics.

## Recording lineage binding

One phone recording (the client id R that the phone sends as `client_conversation_id` and on every WAL) becomes one live row per silence rollover, each with its own server recording id. With `SYNC_LINEAGE_RESOLVE_ENABLED` on (default; unset means on, any unrecognized value means off), `/v4/listen` stamps every generation with `external_data.recording_origin_id = R`, and an upload that carries R plus audio bounds is bound per VAD segment, after VAD, by `recording_lineage.py`. One bounded, indexed read (`conversations_recording_lineage`: R, `started_at <= latest segment end + 5 s` and `finished_at >= earliest segment start`, ordered by start and finish descending, at most 9 metadata-only documents) returns the recording's generations; generations created before the stamp are found only through the row bound to R itself (at most 6 more documents, read only when that row is absent from an untruncated window). A segment binds when the strict pass yields one canonical generation, or when several strictly contain it and the phone's stamp names exactly one of them — by row id or by canonical — preferring the non-deleted canonical row among the matches, else the smallest matching id; canonical uniqueness still wins first and the chosen row resolves through the same donor-redirect path. No local clock reliably orders back-dated rows, so with no compatible stamp the overlap stays undecidable: under the `SYNC_LINEAGE_LIVE_DEDUPE_ENABLED` gate (same allowlist) the segment keeps no target and the `ambiguous_pending` token, is rechecked against a fresh lineage read at most three times per plan, then defers through the existing retryable `sync_persistence_failed` failed-segment path rather than guessing a row; with the gate off the overlap is an ordinary interval miss and the stamp or temporal fallback applies. Failing that, the `recording_session_target` allowance applies (the segment must overlap the generation, may start 5 s before it and end 60 s after its last word). Source, device and lock state must match. Smart-merge donors stay in the lineage and canonicalize to their survivor; when only the donor is visible, intake follows its redirect and a deleted survivor supersedes the audio. Every potentially overlapping row is considered, including an old survivor or stamp-extended row. A truncated overlap set proves no target; the whole plan falls back rather than assuming older rows cannot overlap. Zero, negative or non-finite audio spans also prove no target. The document-read ceiling is 16 (typically a few overlapping generations plus the legacy origin lookup); multi-range index-entry reads are billed separately and have no measured ceiling here. When a clean lookup — not degraded, failed or truncated — returns zero rows and the live-dedupe gate admits the uid, one extra metadata-only document read addressed by the recording id itself runs once and classifies that exact row as `missing`, `unstamped`, `source`, `device`, `lock`, `deleted`, `interval` or `compatible` (`id_probe`); it never joins the targets, changes fallback or recreates anything, and `missing` only proves absence at read time, not whether the row raced the index or was never written. With the dedupe switch off or the uid outside the cohort the probe never runs and the resolve line keeps the pre-diagnostics field set, restoring main reads and log bytes exactly.

A bound segment is an explicit target through the existing `assign_in_transaction` path, so live-target dedupe, identity retention, `sync_live_target` and user-managed protections are unchanged. A unique generation different from the phone's stamp overrides it. Without a unique generation the segment keeps the stamp (the pre-#19424 behavior) or, when unstamped, stays on temporal assignment, which never adopts live rows — except a strict multi-canonical overlap under the gate, which keeps no target and the pending token instead of guessing, and a key once proven pending stays pending across the plan's rechecks unless a later pass binds it. The plan is a pure function of rows, segment spans and stamp; retries skip landed segments through the processed-segment ledgers and bind the rest the same way against the same rows. If the lineage query fails (for example while its composite index is still building), the origin-row read still runs, so binding degrades to the whole-batch resolver's candidate set, per segment (`window=origin_row_only`). Any other lookup or planning error, including span construction or executor failure at the coordinator boundary, falls back to the stamp or temporal path and never fails the job. Each decision emits `omi_sync_lineage_resolve_total{outcome}` and one `event=sync_lineage_resolve` log line with segment counts and no ids; outcomes are `bound`, `split_across_generations`, `stamp_overridden`, `stamp_fallback`, `no_rows`, `truncated`, `interval_miss`, `lookup_failed`, `disabled`, `not_allowlisted`. When the live-dedupe gate admits the uid the line also carries per-binding counts and stage counts over the returned candidates only — `candidates`, `accepted`, `dropped_invalid|unstamped|deleted|source|device|lock|interval` and `unstamped_origin`, each capped at 16 at emission while the plan keeps truthful totals; these are not the recording's total inventory, and a degraded, failed or truncated lookup leaves the cause unknown. Off, the whole batch resolves as before: only a row whose recording id is R, containing the whole upload, binds, and a miss drops the stamp.

With the flag on, sync enrichment defers while an explicit live target is `in_progress`, so SYNC_UPDATE cannot prematurely complete an open recording. Finalization processes it once live closes. A fresh live speech write to a sync-enriched target advances `sync_content_revision` atomically with the changed transcript; a processor that read earlier is fenced. An unchanged live retry leaves the revision intact. Sync intake also preserves a live row's durable `audio_timeline` first-audio origin: rebasing it for an early WAL would shift later live offsets. Buffered speech before that pin keeps its exact negative relative timestamp and is marked `audio_alignment=unplaced`, because released clients cannot seek negative v2 offsets; its text is retained. Completed targets still reprocess per finishing job. On retry, durable append receipts restore pending enrichment even when every landed segment is skipped, excluding fenced targets and retaining the stored conversation language. Retries can repeat enrichment; this is not an exactly-once LLM guarantee. OFF leaves these prior lifecycle behaviors intact.

### Staged rollout (uid allowlist)

`SYNC_LINEAGE_RESOLVE_UID_ALLOWLIST` (comma-separated uids, whitespace-trimmed; empty or unset admits every user) gates, by the owning uid, every sync-side behavior the kill switch turns on: the per-segment binding request (`lineage_resolution_requested`, independent durable refresh debt survives later narrowing), the pinned live origin in `assign_in_transaction` (uid = `user_ref.id`), the live `sync_content_revision` advance in `database/conversations.update_conversation_segments` (backend-listen), and the open-live enrichment deferral in `_reprocess_conversation_after_update`. The gate is `config/sync_lineage.sync_lineage_resolve_active_for`: the kill switch wins, then the list. For a uid outside a non-empty list each of those sites behaves exactly as with the kill switch off. The `/v4/listen` origin stamp is metadata only and stays under the kill switch alone, so widening later finds lineage already stamped. An eligible upload refused by the list records `outcome=not_allowlisted` on the metric and log line with `job_ref=none` (no uid, job or conversation id).

`SYNC_LINEAGE_S1_REQUIRED` (default on; unset or blank means on, explicit on-tokens mean on, any unrecognized value including a typo means off) adds a claim gate inside `lineage_resolution_requested`, after the kill switch and the allowlist. With the gate on, per-segment binding additionally requires `CAPTURE_EVIDENCE_V1_DARK_WRITE` admission plus a complete validated S1 claim set for the exact uploaded basename set, checked through the existing `parse_sync_file_claims` bounds (at most 20 files, bounded JSON, matching root/epoch/rate/channel/codec). An absent, invalid, incomplete, wrong-filename or otherwise malformed claim set returns false and the upload takes the pre-lineage whole-batch resolver — even for admitted users and with an empty allowlist. Each refusal emits exactly one bounded `s1_refused` outcome on `OMI_SYNC_LINEAGE_RESOLVE_TOTAL` plus one log line with a closed reason (`no_claims`, `parse_failed`, `count_mismatch`, `filename_mismatch`, `not_admitted`), `job_ref=none`, and no ids, filenames, roots or claim content; claims rejected at the upload HTTP boundary arrive as `{}` and classify as `no_claims` because the queued shape does not retain the earlier refusal. After decode and coverage, a second check at the coordinator disables lineage and resolves the same whole-batch target when the observed source-position mapping count differs from the surviving WAV set, emitting `count_mismatch` once before that resolution; valid decoded prefixes still count as observed mappings, and a fully committed suppression leaves no segments to bind. No new policy runs inside `process_segment`, so direct legacy callers keep the old contract. Turning the gate off restores prior ungated lineage binding and thereby broadens eligibility; it is not a lineage kill switch. The gate never widens the uid allowlist and never bypasses `SYNC_LINEAGE_RESOLVE_ENABLED`. Deployment declares the value `true` on Cloud Run `backend`, `backend-sync`, `backend-sync-backfill` and `backend-integration`.

The gate uses the service env and the row's owning uid; the env is fixed per revision/pod. Landed segments stay skipped, and a changed allowlist controls only the remaining appends. An admitted append checkpoints `lineage_enrichment_pending` before its processed-segment marker, in both the job and durable content receipts. Narrowing the list or turning the kill switch off must still finish that existing refresh debt for a completed row, using its stored language, without reappending audio. Fenced targets are excluded. A retry refresh of an open live row still defers to live finalization even after narrowing. This is completion of an already admitted effect, not a pinned permission for new appends; untouched refused owners retain the exact OFF outcome. Pre-marker receipts restore under an admitted attempt only. Widening may put remaining segments on different rows, but never rebinds landed speech. Avoid changing membership while a user's live recording or retry job is running: mixed revisions can disagree on pin preservation and revision fencing. Drain first when narrowing; disabling does not undo doubled speech.

Residual overlap: a WAL is uploaded whole when any frame missed the socket, so bound segments repeat speech live already transcribed. With `SYNC_LINEAGE_LIVE_DEDUPE_ENABLED` on (unset means on, any unrecognized value means off, and the lineage uid allowlist still gates it), intake first asks `capture_repeat_evidence.py` which incoming segments carry independent capture proof: each segment's own `sync_vad` receipt must uniquely identify a nonempty source-frame span (unique within the intake — a duplicated incoming or receipt id proves neither side), and the canonical live row's `origin=live` S1 snapshot must positively cover every touched frame for the same capture root, clock epoch, mono channel and rate, with valid endpoint sample offsets and `conflicts=0`; missing, malformed, partial or mismatched evidence yields no proof. Only a proven segment may drop, and only when its normalized tokens (NFKC, casefolded, punctuation-neutral) fit, multiplicity-included and minus a fixed filler set, inside one contiguous window of one to three chronologically adjacent live segments (gaps at most 15 s): difflib ordered-token matches must cover at least 85% of incoming and 75% of window tokens, at least two distinct shared trigrams are required, the duration ratio must sit inside 0.5-2.0, and the midpoints must be within 1800 s of each other. Live windows resolve on `audio_capture_start/end` or, for the v2 pinned timeline, the pinned origin plus offsets; otherwise `started_at` plus offsets, and WAL timestamps are never rewritten from text. A repeat never drops on time coverage alone — and never on lexical match alone: an unproven segment, even an identical second utterance, is legitimate repetition and is kept before any candidate work. Protected tokens (negations, correction cues and any token containing a digit) must match the window exactly, and every other substantive incoming token must be counter-included, so novel words, numbers, negation changes and correction markers keep the whole segment; filler differences are tolerated. The pass abstains conservatively: with no verified indices, or more than 4096 stored live segments, nothing is scanned or tokenized; a midpoint-sorted live index is built once and bisected to the ±1800 s neighborhood, and a neighborhood over 128 candidates keeps the segment before any candidate sort or tokenization. After lexical coverage a safe exact-retry pass drops only identical normalized text at an identical absolute range or segment id — and only for a capture-proven segment or one sharing a nonempty `sync:` speaker scope with the stored line; exact retries against unproven live text, ranges or bare ids are kept, and a canonical row over 4096 stored segments returns every incoming segment unchanged rather than suppressing against a truncated pool. An upload whose survivors are all repeats acknowledges with no writes, index changes, donor tombstones or revision bump — an overlapping donor never forces consolidation or rollover of an all-repeat intake; enrichment is suppressed unless the dropped speech was a canonical sync-scoped exact retry — that intake completes previously admitted work (`existing_completion=True` on the append line, the owed debt enrolled, finish invoked without new audio), while pristine live repeats and `sync_merged_from` ancestry alone invent no debt; explicitly owed `_merged` debt remains. Failure modes: paraphrase or synonym rewording misses the lexical match and stays doubled, a legitimately repeated long phrase can drop in error, and there is no guarantee production lands under the 10% goal above. Strict-overlap ambiguity keeps no target and the `ambiguous_pending` token rather than guessing a row; a compatible phone stamp disambiguates it, and the listen side never clamps `started_at`, a spec divergence from the pinned `audio_timeline` origin. Literal stamp fallback can still stretch a row across a real gap and cause later ambiguity, as it did before #19424. Telemetry is content-free: `event=sync_lineage_append` carries `span_delta_bucket`, the chosen row's date-extent growth bucketed `none|0|0_60|60_300|gt_300|unknown` — a repeat-only no-op reports `0`, and a size rollover onto a new sync row is never attributed to live stretch; `event=sync_lineage_stamp_append` marks stamp-fallback live appends while the gate is on. With dedupe off the intake writes no markers and emits neither line, restoring the main persistence and telemetry exactly. Historical captures, clock-offset calibration, uploads that bind to no row and exactly-once enrichment are not solved here.

Frame-level audio coverage: lexical dedupe can only drop repeats a VAD segment still carries, so WALs that mostly repeat live speech still pay full STT. With `SYNC_WAL_AUDIO_COVERAGE_ENABLED` on (unset or blank means on, any unrecognized value means off, and the lineage uid allowlist still gates it), the upload path joins the opt-in S1 source-position evidence: each decoded WAL file's upload claim (`capture_root`, `clock_epoch`, mono channel, `rate_hz`, source-frame start/count) is matched against `capture_evidence` snapshots on provenance-compatible rows from the same bounded lineage read, and `utils/sync/audio_coverage.py` removes exactly the source frames a committed live transcript proof covers, before VAD/STT. Only `version=1`, `capability=source_position`, `origin=live`, `proof=committed_transcript_v1`, `coverage` mapped or incomplete, `conflicts=0` envelopes donate proof — receipt-only snapshots carry no `proof` and abstain — and `utils/sync/committed_coverage.py` additionally requires the envelope's bounded lifetime history (version 1, complete, conflict-free, at most 16 root/epoch entries) to contain every committed run's ordinals and receipt window. The check is negative and clock-based: the run's receipt wall anchors must independently reproduce the decoded WAL's actual basename-timestamp clock and uniform frame geometry within 2 s, so a stale or competing same-root lifetime (overlapping source ranges with disjoint receipt windows) abstains the whole batch rather than guessing; time agreement alone never proves a frame received. A same-root/epoch envelope with nonzero conflicts, malformed matching runs, a wrong type on `rate_hz`, more than 32 runs per envelope or more than 13 envelopes all abstain the whole batch, and unknown, unmapped or mismatched envelopes contribute nothing. Recall stays conservative — in-socket ordinal resets are caught by the producer high-water, while very close cross-socket resets additionally depend on the independent app root-rotation release rather than any cryptographic proof. Kept intervals expand by at most 0.25 s of whole adjacent frames per side as bounded context: the margin may re-include already live-received edge frames (a small bounded duplicate — context is not zero-duplication) and may span a small covered hole where two expanded intervals overlap, but it never concatenates non-contiguous captures beyond that bound, so an upload writes per-interval derivative WAVs under a `<stem>.coverage/` sibling directory named with the original phone-clock start plus sample offset, each carrying a rebased `source_frame_map`. Split derivatives carry an internal `coverage_trimmed` marker that lets the 1 s VAD minimum-span floor admit genuinely new sub-second slices; unmarked audio keeps the floor unchanged. Abstention at any step — no decoded map, lookup degraded or truncated, no matching evidence, invalid WAV geometry — keeps the original bytes, path and map; an upload with no S1 claim never reaches the consumer, costs no lookup and emits no log. A fully covered file skips VAD/STT entirely and the job ledger records success rather than expected silence. One bounded `event=sync_wal_audio_coverage` line reports kept/dropped/context seconds, file count and suppressed count with no ids; ordinary failures report the shared `record_fallback` (`from=sync_lineage to=temporal reason=other outcome=degraded`) and retain everything. Cancellation mid-split preserves the pre-handoff inputs like decode does, abandons the derivative adoption, and any generated files already tracked are removed on internal error; the consumer never deletes inputs it did not create. The lexical receipt matcher is disabled on this production seam: it is invoked with no WAL context with which to prove live ranges, so only committed frame proof suppresses audio — exact sync-scoped retries at intake remain unchanged, and the bounded context margin conservatively preserves repeats.

Offline replay: `scripts/sync_live_dedupe_replay.py` replays supplied STT candidates through the same `capture_repeat_evidence`/`live_speech_dedupe`/`audio_coverage` primitives with no env, network or provider imports, for incident investigation against a local JSON. The input carries `live_segments` and `sync_segments` (absolute `start`/`end` seconds plus `text`), optional `live_capture_evidence`/`sync_capture_evidence` envelopes in the stored shapes, an `intake_sizes` pattern of successive intakes (integers 2 through 5, default `[2,3,4,5]`, expanded so a two-or-more-segment remainder never leaves a trailing one-segment intake), and an optional `audio_coverage` block (`live_received_ranges` envelopes and `wal_frames` descriptions with `capture_root`, `clock_epoch`, `source_frame_start`, `rate_hz`, `codec` and decoded `frame_samples`, plus an optional declared `frame_count` that must be at least the decoded prefix length) that runs the production planner and reports per-file kept/dropped/context seconds and kept frame ranges. Each intake feeds fresh `sync:replay:<n>` scopes and prunes supplied `sync_vad` receipts to its batch; a candidate whose source-frame span straddles kept and removed audio is retained whole and marked `partial_audio` — transcript totals are supplied-candidate outcomes, not an estimate of post-trim STT recall. One inherited production limitation is modeled exactly: an appending intake persists merged `sync_vad` receipts over the live row's `origin=live` run envelope, so a later intake loses the positive live proof and keeps repeats it would otherwise have dropped. Malformed input exits 2 with a generic message and never leaks a path, id or text. Each WAL may additionally supply wal_start_seconds as the offline description of its observed decoded WAV basename timestamp; committed proofs require this context and frame_samples, while an absent wall anchor retains audio.

## Relevance and remaining differences

Speech the deterministic relevance rules (`utils/conversations/relevance_rules.py`) discard outright gets `sync_relevance=review` and skips enrichment; everything else is assessed by the relevance step as a `SYNC_UPDATE` when the pipeline processes it. Uncurated completed review fragments are stored `discarded=True`: hidden from default lists/search, retained with their transcript/audio, and recoverable through Show discarded. Readers trust that stored flag alone; `scripts/conversation_relevance_backfill.py` rewrites rows stored before intake did so. Explicit restoration of any row sets `sync_relevance_user_kept` so later intake and reassessment honor that choice. Curated, shared, photo-bearing, and live-target records are protected; a generated summary is not curation and protects nothing. Duration alone never discards meaningful speech. Subsequent intake reassesses the complete transcript. Unknown content/language stays `keep`; speaker profiles and `is_user` never gate assignment or relevance.

Known limitation: WALs carrying different existing live target IDs can remain separate even during one continuous recording. Sync never bridges those live-owned targets because an open socket may still write to them; partial-flap intake does not guarantee partition parity. Realtime now remembers same-origin continuations inside the continuity window and reaps expired empty generations on reconnect. This prevents one source of new target proliferation; it does not redirect historical distinct explicit live targets. Recording lineage binding (below) covers uploads that carry a recording id; historical rows are not migrated.

The shared arithmetic does not unify the observed clocks: live finished_at is currently callback wall time, while sync uses VAD/word ends. Another boundary-policy input difference is timeout configuration: realtime can configure its timeout per session; shipped WALs do not carry that setting, so sync uses the default 120 seconds. Tests replay the in-order realtime speech rule as the reference and require identical sync membership, absolute transcript ranges and extents across arrival permutations for temporal intake, including 119-second joins and >=120-second splits with hint-only live stubs and missing target IDs present. Sync enrichment remains per finishing job and revision-fenced, not quiescence-debounced. Historical aliases already returned to clients may require a history refresh. Tests model serial commit permutations and replay failures hermetically; Firestore retry contention and live UI acceptance require separate evidence.

## Assignment outcomes

Labeled non-target donors are excluded before extending the capture interval. A labeled
row may remain the survivor for temporal appends, but never donate its receipt into
another speaker namespace. Retargeting a labeled retry anchor is permanent supersession.

Deleted anchors, deleted redirect lineage and user-managed or labeled retargeted retry anchors raise
`SyncAssignmentSuperseded`. `process_segment` consumes them quietly with no error,
content usage or response IDs. It returns false, letting sibling segments proceed;
a zero-error job commits the content-completion ledger before publishing completed
(inline and Cloud Tasks). The bounded log is `sync_assignment outcome=superseded`;
this does not masquerade as speech silence. Provenance mismatches and redirect cycles
raise `SyncAssignmentConflict` and remain loud retryable upstream errors under the
existing job error taxonomy. Copy/checkpoint failures also retain retry behavior.
Donor retraction that collides with the account destructive-operation gate is
deferred and converges on a later append; it does not fail the accepted chunk.

## Speaker identity

Speaker IDs are conversation-local. Each independently transcribed WAL chunk
carries a retry-stable content scope (legacy direct intake uses capture time).
After duplicate removal, the transaction hydrates the surviving conversation's
allocator and allocates incoming and donor identities. Legacy donors receive a
stable conversation/speaker scope. Provider labels and recognized person IDs are
preserved; equal provider numbers never establish that two voices are the same.
Conversation-wide resolution uses stored private-cloud audio after each sync
job's append and reprocess. If audio is unavailable or its timeline cannot be
trusted, the resolver marks the cross-chunk IDs `unavailable` and logs the
anonymous skip reason; clients must treat those labels as provisional. A sync
bridge's redirect tombstone remains available to owner detail reads and manual
speaker assignment: the assignment transaction follows the survivor chain and
maps the donor's selected speaker number through stable segment IDs so it never
labels a different survivor voice.
