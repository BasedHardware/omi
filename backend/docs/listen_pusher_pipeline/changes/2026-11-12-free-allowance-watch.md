# Shared free-allowance watch ledger (SCA-1750)

This implements the watch-only reservation phase. Shared Firestore transactions account for verified monthly transcription billing, outstanding operation holds, and PTT seconds already charged by the existing daily budget. Serving admission, plan limits, prices, and client payloads are unchanged. Enforcement and removal of legacy route checks require a separate authorized rollout.

## Reservation and settlement contract

`database/free_allowance_leases.py` has no flag, cohort, or launch-date gate. Authenticated live, upload, batch, one-shot, and PTT operations carry stable server-owned identities into provider dispatch. Retries, fallback providers, multichannel legs, and replicas share the operation hold. Paid plans and trials on paid plans return before ledger access. Customer-key exemption is decided for the actual provider; managed fallbacks reserve managed spend. Unbound free provider calls produce `billing_scope_unavailable`, never a snapshot-based permission.

Usage must have a verified monthly baseline with the current schema and epoch. A missing/not-ready projection can be established by a successful fenced hourly-source scan; an empty successful scan proves zero. Corrupt ready projections, failed queries, unavailable identity, subscription errors, and deleted or erasing accounts produce refusal observations. None becomes an empty-usage allowance.

Live reservations use the existing billing clock, including idle, held-audio, and resume exclusions. Windows are at most 30 seconds; a final partial window expires at its funded remainder. Renewal uses a separate lifetime task, with cancellation awaited at operation close. Offline requests reserve their existing billing estimate; unknown duration is an explicit refusal observation. Holds are bounded to 128 per month and an operation to three billing months. Expired holds remain charged until trusted settlement: a crash cannot manufacture new allowance. These conservative limits and orphan holds need operational reconciliation before enforcement.

Billing remains authoritative. After its existing write succeeds, the operation joins the exact billed seconds to a durable settlement receipt and releases its holds, including registered prior-month holds. Settlement is a separate idempotent transaction: a lost observer reply can temporarily retain exposure, but cannot undo or retry the billing write. Batch completion replays repair registered settlement gaps. PTT mirrors the exact daily-budget units into the shared ledger without changing existing daily/monthly counters. Zero-charge terminal operations settle zero only after the owning operation proves completion. Root-existence and erasure fences cover new ledger publications.

Batch accounting uses one cumulative aggregate receipt per content billing generation; individual unit receipts carry zero seconds. The existing billing transaction adds observer provenance (`allowance_billed_seconds` and `allowance_billing_started_at`) beside its unchanged hourly updates. The counter adds exactly the writer's integer delta, including zero when native live billing owns the usage. The post-commit observer rereads this counter under the erasure fence and verifies the expected generation. A missing first chunk cannot lose the aggregate; partial/full completion, lost replies, and already-settled chunks converge on one monotonic total. A later full callback can recover an entirely missed partial observer. Old partial ledgers without verified provenance remain unavailable and retain conservative holds; they do not become a zero-valued proof. Reconciliation must use the corrected batch aggregate receipts and exclude pre-fix evidence. Batch aggregates are cumulative snapshots, not per-event deltas: a full-day query must compare window-boundary counters and reconcile the billing source, not attribute the entire total to the latest `settled_at`. The existing receipt TTL declaration covers aggregates too.

Known-zero terminal paths release their hold explicitly: failed one-shot transcription and refunded PTT streams settle zero, including PTT daily-budget fail-open sessions. A completed transcript whose billing write is uncertain retains its hold for reconciliation. There is no blanket expiry sweep that treats an interrupted operation as proof of zero usage.

The processing observer reads this same chargeable balance. The actual summary/memory policy still uses the legacy serving gate until enforcement is authorized. This phase does not implement clean lease-refusal termination or the SCA-1752 live activation condition.

## Instrumentation and reconciliation

The live observer stops and wakes its renewal task when session shutdown begins, before draining session tasks; it cannot extend source-close cleanup. Batch settlement lives in the CPU projection module `utils/stt/allowance_batch.py`, keeping GPU-shared telemetry free of sync/database coordinator dependencies. Emulator correctness proofs await the real observer workers independently of the serving wait limit.

`STT_FREE_ALLOWANCE_WATCH_ENABLED` defaults to `true`; `false`, `0`, `off`, and `no` stop new observations/reservations. Confirmed existing operations may still settle. The switch cannot change a serving admission result. New I/O uses a dedicated bounded executor (two workers, four queued jobs). Async/sync callers wait at most 2.5 seconds; batch admission uses at most 100 ms of its remaining authorization budget after all serving decisions are sealed. Timeout does not prove RPC cancellation; an underlying transaction may finish later.

`omi_free_allowance_watch_decisions` and `omi_free_allowance_watch_seconds` describe requests, retries, renewals, and processing observations. `omi_free_allowance_lease_decisions` labels reservation reasons. Do not sum these durations or paths: they can describe the same work multiple times. `omi_free_allowance_settled_seconds` counts confirmed joins, but a lost reply can leave a metric gap. Only `allowance_exhausted` is an allowance `would_refuse`. `already_settled` emits its own decision with zero seconds; `lease_expired`, `ledger_full`, and `operation_too_old` retain their diagnostic labels. These outcomes and unreadable usage do not mark a receipt as a measured allowance refusal. A receipt without an observed allowance decision is `unobserved`. Historical watch receipts from earlier code must not be used for the full-day gate. Durable operation receipts, joined to aggregate settled billing/unit-cost data, are the reconciliation authority. Receipts retain bounded source and decision labels; only one primary month receipt carries a non-batch operation's billed seconds; batch totals belong to the generation aggregate. Logs/metric labels contain no UID, transcript, or audio.

`backend/deploy/free-allowance-retention.json` declares 90-day receipt and settled/unreserved-operation expiry. Active operations protecting holds have no TTL. The release owner can review the existing TTL-only operator with `python backend/scripts/batch_pipeline_ttl.py --project <project> --policy backend/deploy/free-allowance-retention.json`; its default mode only prints a plan. This declaration is not applied by this change and must be explicitly applied before production activation. Normal recursive account deletion must include these user subcollections.

## Validation and rollout gates

Unit and loopback Firestore emulator tests cover concurrency, operation replay, transaction abort/retry, billing-unit settlement, PTT replay, baseline proof/query failure, month rollover, and erasure fences. Contention may exhaust the bounded transaction attempts; that is unavailable, never an extra grant. Local tests cannot prove production coverage, latency, cost, or settled excess hours.

Before enforcement: release the watch-only phase through the release owner, reconcile one full day of aggregate observations and durable receipts against settled excess hours on every route, inspect unknown-identity/duration and orphan-hold coverage, and measure Firestore/latency cost. Then separately authorize offline enforcement, the shared processing gate, and legacy-check removal. Live clean stopping requires the SCA-1752 on-device switch. Any user-visible change needs renders and David's approval. No deployment, production account reads, credential changes, or measured savings are part of this implementation.
